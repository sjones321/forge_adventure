# Shandalar Next — Base, Hall, MTG & Automation Design Notes
Updated 2026-10-16. Captures conversational design decisions; proposals and open questions explicitly marked. Newer decisions supersede older Fortress-Design-Brief where they conflict.

## World and construction
- Surface world is persistent, with freeform building anywhere outside protected radii around points of interest. No mandatory land-claim flag. Special Bellwarden-related construction can occur inside towns.
- Underground consists of many descending layers, natural caverns, blockages and resources. Digging/excavation primarily happens underground; danger increases with depth. Surface is home to settlements, farms, workshops, etc.
- Multiple bases, workshops, mining outposts and settlements are allowed. Only one Hall of Remembrance transplanar core per run/player.
- Bases remain physically intact when the core moves. Core-dependent functions stop, and can resume if the core returns. Moving is not penalized; duplicating supporting infrastructure at the destination is the practical cost.

## Hall of Remembrance (HoR)
- Sacred Death Watch preservation relic: an expandable extradimensional Hall *inside* a nearly indestructible transplanar core/orb, not the physical building. Only extraordinarily powerful cosmic beings (Trueborn-tier) could erase it. It grows as needed; securely recording contents takes time, not finite physical capacity.
- Install core into a physical housing/casing that functions as the doorway. The housing needs ordinary power (technological or magical) to operate. A housing plus generator or battery can establish temporary access anywhere; a developed base provides lasting infrastructure.
- Core/Hall is stable without external power. Hall produces a finite steady amount of *transplanar energy* (TP) as a byproduct of its preservation function, with some storage and internal regulation. Conventional power opens the housing; TP powers special systems.
- Starting Hall has basic living amenities/storage but no meaningful production. First away mission, minimal supervision; personal archive/village nooks start essentially empty. No inherent prohibition on decoration, industry, or personal rooms inside Hall, but manufacturing is not its sacred purpose.
- Hall preserves records *outside causal reality*; the Nothing erases worlds and their causal history, but cannot erase transplanar Hall records. Recording before erasure is crucial; recovering erased people afterward is extraordinarily difficult.
- A complete villager record requires a soul print. Taking one without informed consent is technologically possible but a severe ethical/legal breach, answerable in the Between where everyone is genuinely equal regardless of power or soul condensation. Local culture may lack vocabulary for the true technology, so explain in terms native to their multiverse without deceiving them.
- Early game: earn individuals' trust and permission one by one. Later: popularity, Bellwardens, advanced locally adapted infrastructure, and real improvements in village life support broader communal preservation.
- Each fully remembered villager increases Hall capacity and consequently available TP as an incidental byproduct; not treating people as fuel.
- Normal powered housing is persistent Hall access. Rare single-use beacon can remotely pull player into Hall from *any* distance, depth, or plane, even Prime Universe One, provided Hall housing is powered. Other fast travel is preferred to conserve beacon resources.
- TP uses: short-range connected base storage (crafting from chests, quick stack); bigger inventory bags with specialized capacity (e.g. one-slot bag for 10 stacks of bones); deep storage. Stored transplanar objects stay stable without TP; only access needs energy.
- Hall can extend a spherical transplanar cloak around base, phasing terrain/buildings/residents out of outside reality perception and interaction. Sophisticated intelligence permits exceptions, e.g. landmarks remain visible and no absurd spherical void in ground. Bigger radius costs more TP.
- Base automation/switches can shed nonessential TP uses and redirect power to cloak in emergencies. Finite TP output + reserve creates real tradeoffs.

## MTG in the world
- This is a multiversal cluster bound to Earth, potentially encompassing Doctor Who, Marvel, DC, Star Wars etc.; no need to slavishly reproduce all canonical MTG history. Cards are real supernatural power in this cluster, akin to Yu-Gi-Oh! anime. Ordinary citizens often have only one or two cards, wealthy/powerful people whole decks.
- Villagers teach **card knowledge/schematics**, not hand over their treasured physical card. Once learned, player can craft permanent copies. Cards are NOT consumed by playing duels.
- Crafting needs quality-dependent physical materials AND matching rarity essence. Essence can be refined or obtained more easily by salvaging cards. Salvaging an unknown card also teaches its schematic; known cards still yield essence every time.
- Auto-salvage is configurable by card **keep quantity**, not a fixed four-copy playset. Examples: 4 Lightning Bolts, 50 Relentless Rats, 0 unwanted cards, 12 for several decks. Provide bulk/default rules.
- Mastering all printings/alternate arts of a card gives +50% **auto-salvage** yield for that card.
- Each saved deck commits its OWN physical copies; cards cannot be shared simultaneously between decks. Cards assigned to any deck are protected from auto-salvage. Manual salvage of deck-assigned cards needs explicit warning/confirmation or prior removal. Decks can be broken up freely, returning cards to collection; direct card transfers between decks allowed, with incomplete decks flagged.
- Progression links relationships -> card knowledge -> mining/refining/crafting -> collection/decks; base industry supports mass card production and trade, potentially enormous artifact-construct armies.

## Conjures and summoning automation
- Card conjures have meaningful physical presence outside duels. Living creature conjures have free will outside duel arenas; arena binds them to battle rules. Artifact constructs can work, guard and fight outside duels.
- Player can imprint job knowledge into conjures; no need to program every worker manually. Capabilities still depend on actual creature. Planeswalker cards: locals generally get conjures; Death Watch initiate can potentially call *real* planeswalkers, with diplomatic/location-exposure risks. This is distinct from ordinary creature conjures.
- For base work, slot a physical artifact creature card into a summoning device; it manifests that construct, tying up the card (unavailable for decks). No arbitrary worker/combat separation. Device needs large initial summoning power burst, then small continuous upkeep.
- If power fails, conjures destabilize gradually over **one in-game day**; repowering in time restores them; after sustained outage they dissipate. Slotted cards remain safe and can resummon when power returns.
- If conjure is killed, its card remains safe **unless the physical summoning machine holding the card is destroyed**. Destruction of the machine risks card loss.
- In principle even Emrakul can be conjured with extreme late-game power. Still a conjure, not original entity; can fade if sustaining power cut. Outside arena, catastrophic creature behavior may kill player before that happens (potential game over). Other powerful creatures need not behave identically.
- Summoning devices have an operating radius. Network extenders allow remote mining outposts without complete bases. Progression: early hardwired connections, later wireless summoning relays.
- Wired connections can carry power as well as summoning connectivity. Wireless summoning arrives before wireless power, requiring local generation or wired supply until wireless power tech unlocked. Wireless power has bandwidth/throughput limits.
- Transmission bandwidth constrains **both construct upkeep and machines** drawing power from the main base. Cables have capacity but **no simulated distance resistance/loss**; avoid excess realism. Wired retains utility at scale.
- Magic energy is more efficient for summons; technological power for machines; later magitech combines strengths but is more expensive/advanced to establish. Neither early branch should become obsolete.
- CONFIRMED: Magical and technological power use **separate networks** initially. Magitech progression enables integration/hybrid networks.

## Pending question (ask ONE at a time)
- CONFIRMED: Magitech batteries accept input from both mana and technological electricity and produce a **new unified energy output usable by all machines**, including existing magical and technological devices. Both energy sources must contribute, but the ratio is flexible in either direction: e.g. 10% mana / 90% electricity OR 90% mana / 10% electricity. A small ongoing magical contribution is required, and likewise some technological input is needed; neither must dominate.
- CONFIRMED: Unified magitech output does not automatically stack both power-type efficiency bonuses. Ordinary machines retain the bonus appropriate to their own specialization (magical or technological); only high-end magitech machines can benefit from **both** bonuses.
- CONFIRMED: Technology acquisition uses a dedicated research system. Consulting a relevant expert NPC grants a substantial research bonus, provisionally ~50% reduction in resource cost OR research time (which one depends on the research mechanics yet to be designed). This preserves NPC/social progression as a meaningful advantage.
- CONFIRMED: Research progression is hybrid in *acquisition routes*, not just cost/time: expert NPCs may give research discounts or speed bonuses; exploration discoveries such as hidden archives may grant other bonuses or even unlock an entire technology outright. Do not make every tech require the same discovery path.
- CONFIRMED: Baseline research requires BOTH material inputs (experimentation/prototypes) and elapsed time (investigation). Experts and discoveries can reduce requirements; complete archives may bypass them.

- WORLD GENERATION (CONFIRMED): New runs begin in Havenbrook, a welcoming village at the center of the map. Five primary outward colored biome domains correspond to White, Blue, Black, Red, and Green. CORRECTION: The colorless Wastes are not a sixth wedge; they lie along the outer edge of EVERY colored biome. CONFIRMED: Starting plane 1 (Havenbrook) has a consistent overall layout and five monocolor domains, with only minor procedural variations between runs. Build a reusable biome palette based on MTG color identities: five mono-, ten two-, ten three-, five four-, and one five-color combinations (31 nonempty colored identities total), plus colorless Wastes/possible other colorless biomes. Each subsequent plane corresponds to an MTG set and emphasizes biomes that reflect that set's themes and color identities; multi-color biomes can be dominant regions, not merely borders. This conveys the feel of each set WITHOUT rebuilding its canonical setting (e.g., no requirement to recreate Ravnica). Color identity is a design palette, not a claim that guilds/factions physically exist on all planes. Biome families can contain multiple environment types and plane-specific visual variants; example: G emphasizes forests, while UG can support jungles. Art production is a primary project constraint: seek variety through reusable assets, palettes, lighting, density, and selective distinctive set dressing, avoiding runaway art scope.
