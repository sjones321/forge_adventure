# Lore Bible — the Eternal Engine

The setting is Steve's own original cosmology, **the Eternal Engine**. The Magic: The Gathering multiverse appears
inside it as one bubble pattern among many. Lore text is Steve's IP; the game's code is GPL. Keep real people out
of the game.

The full canon lives in Steve's cosmology file (`A:\Eternal_Engine_World_Building\lore\`). This doc is the slice
the game needs. If the two disagree, the cosmology file wins.

## The cosmology (what the game needs)

- **The Eternal Engine** contains countless **bubble universes**. Each bubble is infinite in local possibility but
  bounded by its core variables. Only decisions of real moral, spiritual or cosmic weight make new bubbles.
- A **serialized bubble pattern** is a collection of bubbles that share a connecting theme. Colloquially, a
  "multiverse". The Magic multiverse is one such pattern; each **plane** in the game is a bubble in it.
- **Travel is lateral, never backward.** True backward time travel always collapses. Moving between bubbles is
  lateral travel, which is what planar portals do.
- **Time runs in gravity wells**: local, regional, planetary, galactic and up. Deeper wells bend time harder.
  (Game use: delve depth.)

## The powers

- **The Firstborn**: the first and greatest beings of the Engine. There are twenty (the number can grow). Their
  order is **both birth order and power**.
  - **#1 The Emperor.** Never acts personally and has never rebirthed. Sits an endless vigil on his throne,
    protective of what he views as his people. Will readily send enough force to neutralize even a Fallen.
  - **#2 The leader of the Death Watch.** Born shortly after the Emperor, so any power gap is minimal; the
    difference is that this one fights on the front lines. Arguably the most powerful souled being, counting both
    raw power and the forces at their command. Strictly apolitical, but the Fallen aren't political, so they get
    crushed.
  - Others relevant to the game: **#4 Knowledge** (whose greater aspect Science covers magic and technology alike)
    and **#6 Memory**.
  - **The Fallen**: the **last three Firstborn**, corrupted. Being the weakest of the Firstborn is how they could
    be corrupted. They must act in secret, because open action draws the other Firstborn down on them.
- **The Intrinsics** stay out of things unless it's a very big deal.

## The Death Watch

- The Engine's elite military and **cosmic immune system**, but above all its **Archivists**. The Nothing's only
  power is subtraction, so **memory is the counterattack**. When a civilization falls, the Death Watch records it.
- **What the archives keep is culture first.** Facts, yes, and archives are examined for knowledge the Watch lacks,
  but those hits are rare. The point is who these people were.
- A million years minimum training, a billion years minimum service, protected from death while serving.
- **Ranks**, by condensation:
  - **Watchers** master Time and Memory. They acquire data.
  - **Engineers** sit a few steps of condensation above Watchers and must master Science: magic, technology,
    engineering, biology, anything constructed. Just as formidable as Knights, in a different specialty.
  - **Knights** are the truly condensed. Rare.

### The Harmonic Bell

- The pivotal thing a Death Watch contingent does against the Nothing. A **field with limited range**: inside it,
  a soul the Nothing reaches **loses its connection and immediately transitions home** instead of being unmade.
- It doesn't break the Nothing's power; it wins the race.
- The contingent can sustain Bells indefinitely, but there are only so many of them, so Engineers must anchor each
  Bell with **rituals at key sites**.

## The player

- You are a **Death Watch Initiate**. By most standards, Initiates in their million years of training already
  count as ultra-elite. Once fully **blooded and tattooed**, an Initiate is **bonded with their Watcher** and sent
  on missions; missions are the training.
- **Why you're on your own:** a full Death Watch contingent *is* in this multiverse, but it is consumed by
  containing the Nothing's spread and holding the Bells. That's why an Initiate is sent in to meddle with the locals
  and find the source.
- Each Initiate has their own **Watcher**, named by the player (with a default name offered, so naming is
  optional). Narratively the Watcher is a spy, advisor and scout: it scouts ahead, gathers intelligence and advises.
  (Game use: the guide and intel voice, scouting reports, hints on where to investigate.)
- **The Watcher's voice grows with you.** It starts precise, literal and a little alien: it reports what it sees,
  not what it means. But you're bonded for a billion years, and it learns from you. Over the run its reports
  shift, and nobody announces it. Early: *"Settlement erased. No survivors recovered."* Hundreds of hours later,
  about a town you loved: *"Millbrook is gone. I'm sorry. I know you liked the smith."* One day you notice it talks
  like someone who knows you.
- A **Supervisor** oversees a set number of Initiates. Also nameable by the player, with a default name offered.
  In co-op, both players share one Supervisor (the host's name for the Supervisor is used).
- **The mission**: activity of the Nothing has been detected in the Magic multiverse. You are sent in to find its
  source. You don't know which plane it's on. You must grow strong enough, and investigate enough planes, to find
  the Fallen behind it and wreck their plans.

### The Supervisor

- Oversees a good handful of Initiates: in practice, all the players and their Watchers.
- **Gruff, because he cares.** He knows getting attached is going to hurt. The people you befriend will live and
  die, and you have a billion years ahead of you. Even towns saved by a Bell will die of time. He has watched a
  lot of Initiates learn that, and he'd rather they learn it from him.
- **He lets it slip a few times.** The key beats:
  1. **The first erased village** (almost certainly one you never visited, with nothing in your Archive). The
     Watcher reports it in clean intel-speak. Then the Supervisor comes on, not barking for once, and says the
     village's name: he went and found it. Draft line: *"Write it down. That's all that's left of them now, and
     it's not much. Next time, get there first."*
  2. **The first town you knew is wiped out**, one where you had relationships. You did everything right and
     still lost them. He reminds you that **you did your job, and they will be remembered.**
  3. **A Bell held.** The land is gone, but every soul inside the Bell went home whole. Sad for the people left
     behind, but the Death Watch knows the souls escaped, and in the Eternal Engine that's what matters. The rare
     time he gets to deliver good news.
- **Every beat is scripted for solo and co-op.** Co-op has its own case when only one player knew the town (the
  shared overworld lets players split up).

**Idea:** survivors from outside a Bell's range, or from eaten land, turn up as refugees in the next town over,
grieving. Their culture is in your Archive, and some of them are still around to talk to.

## The enemy: the Nothing

- **The Nothing eats the map.** Where something flourished, there is now literal nothingness: land, towns,
  resources and roads are erased. **Once it's gone, it's gone. There is no recovery.** That permanence is the true
  nastiness of the Nothing. A town isn't faded out; it's **cut**.
- **Its minions are people with a wound.** Becoming a minion means having your soul cut out of you. Not a file
  erased, a hole in the self, ripe for corruption. Minions keep their memories and their loves; the Nothing just
  removes what would have saved them and lets them be who they already were. A cruel person becomes a cruel person
  with a soul-ending weapon. A decent one may stay decent, hollow and leverageable. Some of them may be townsfolk
  you knew.
- They are given power by **one of the Fallen**, who is hiding somewhere in this multiverse.
- **Which Fallen is unknown, by design.** The true identities of the Fallen are known only to the Primals and
  Primordials; when they fell, the minds of souled beings shifted to know them only as what they are now. They may
  have names among themselves, deliberately left unfilled, so any story can use whatever name fits. In this game
  the Fallen stays nameless (or is known only by an epithet the characters give it).
- **The Fallen's plan**: the Nothing's traditional weakness is that it moves between bubbles slowly. The Fallen
  wants to harness this multiverse's **planeswalking** to build a larger network for moving quickly between
  bubbles. Success would remove the Nothing's greatest weakness.
- **The win condition is exposure, not a duel with a god.** A Fallen is far above even a Death Watch planeswalker.
  Your job is to make the Fallen's work too messy to hide: find the source, break their operation, and drag it
  into the open so the Fallen must withdraw or face the other Firstborn (the Emperor's forces, the Death Watch's
  leader).

## How the story unfolds in play

1. **Arrival**: you start exploring planes, building, gathering, getting to know the locals and mastering the first
   Standard set. The Nothing is only rumor and strange signs.
2. **The Nothing arrives**: after the **first Standard set is mastered**, the Nothing starts appearing and
   competing with you for the multiverse. Fronts open; the map starts disappearing.
3. **The investigation**: the Nothing builds network nodes on planes to link them for fast transit. Each plane
   holds clues. Investigating planes, defeating the Nothing's commanders and closing its breaches narrows down
   where the source is.
4. **Exposure**: you find the Fallen's operation and make it impossible to keep secret. The Fallen's plans fail;
   the Engine's greater powers move in.
5. **After — reincarnation as prestige**: you return to the Between, see the whole ledger, and choose to come back.
   You carry condensed capacity forward, not memories. Townsfolk you bonded with across runs may recognize you
   without knowing why.

## The core loop: friends, the Archive and the Bell

This is where the Stardew half of the game and the Nothing meet.

- **Getting to know the townsfolk is your job, not a side system.** As an Initiate you are an Archivist in
  training: every relationship you build records a town's culture (its people, festivals, crafts, stories) in the
  **Archive** at your fortress.
- **What you archive survives.** When the Nothing eats a town, the land is gone forever. What you recorded is not.
- **The Archive is a substation** in your fortress that communicates with **the Archivist**, the Death Watch's mega
  AI (a Dyson sphere around a stabilized star, with its own maintenance society living inside). What you record is
  sent up to something vastly bigger than you.
- **The Hall of Remembrance**: the Archive's physical form, reached from your fortress. See below.
- **Archive knowledge earns Bells.** The contingent can only anchor so many Harmonic Bells, and your Watcher's
  reports decide where they go. A town you truly know is a town you can make the case for. A town inside a Bell
  loses its land but not its people; their souls go home instead of being unmade.
- **You can't save everyone.** The Watcher reports the Nothing's approach in advance ("eight days at current
  pace"), and each season you choose where your time goes. Some towns you'll save. The ones you lose are ones you
  chose, and none of them have to be forgotten.
- **Seasons are Standard rotation.** Each Standard window is a season; planes drift out of alignment as their sets
  age.

### The Hall of Remembrance

The Archive, fast travel and the relationship screen are **one place**.

- **What it is:** a device issued to every Initiate (Engineer-built), which creates a hall in a **pocket
  dimension** reached from your fortress.
- **Co-op:** each player has their own base, so **each player has their own Hall**.
  - **Guest visits (nice-to-have):** a player can walk into their partner's Hall as a guest, look only, no adding.
    Lets a partner show you a town they love, or stand in a lost town's echo together. **Only if it's technically
    clean and stable**; if it needs fragile workarounds, cut it. Wren's call on feasibility.
- **Structure, procedurally infinite:**
  - **The Endless Hall**, with **one door per plane** you have visited.
  - Behind each door, that plane's **room**, with **one alcove per town** on the plane. Each alcove carries its
    town's name.
  - Rooms and alcoves generate procedurally; special towns can get hand-made rooms.
- **Alcoves fill as you build relationships**: keepsakes townsfolk give you, recipes, festival banners, their
  voices. Every person in the town shows up there.
- **Two ways to use a town's alcove:**
  - **Quick:** a quick menu while you're in your fortress. Pick a town and you **merge from your demi-plane into
    main reality** there. Done quickly, that's fast travel.
  - **Through the Hall:** step into the town's **echo**, a ghostly version of the town (like another plane). Walk
    it, find your people, see your relationship with each, and add your progress. Then merge into reality, or
    return to the Hall.
- **When the Nothing takes a town**, its alcove is **sealed**: nothing more can ever be added, because there is no
  one left to learn from. Fast travel there is gone, because there's no *there*. But **the echo remains**: you can
  still walk the memory of the town, the streets as they were, your friends where they used to stand, frozen at
  the last moment you knew them. "They will be remembered," made walkable.
- **Bell-saved towns** get their own mark in the Hall: their people went home whole.
- Over a long playthrough the Hall becomes a physical history of your run: who you saved, who you lost, and who
  you never reached in time.
- **Build note:** an echo reuses the town's real map with a ghostly filter and a snapshot of its people, so it is
  not a second town to build.

## Gameplay consequences

- **Permanent erasure**: voided land never comes back. The Nothing only advances while you play, its pace is
  tunable, and closing a breach stops that breach's spread. New set planes keep arriving, so the game never runs
  out of world, but each plane you lose is lost for good.
- **The network is the target**: the Nothing's transit nodes near planar portals are the main objective on each
  plane. Left alone, linked nodes let the Nothing jump to new planes faster; destroying them yields clues and
  slows the spread.
- **The Watcher** is the player's guide and intel source (named at character creation).
- **Fortresses anchor reality**: the Nothing can only besiege a fortress, never erase it. The land around it can
  still be eaten.
