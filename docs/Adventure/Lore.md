# Lore Bible — the Eternal Engine

The setting is Steve's own original cosmology, **the Eternal Engine**. The Magic: The Gathering multiverse appears
inside it as one bubble pattern among many. Lore text is Steve's IP; the game's code is GPL. Keep real people out
of the game.

## The cosmology (what the game needs)

- **The Eternal Engine** contains countless **bubble universes**. Each bubble is infinite in local possibility but
  bounded by its core variables. Only decisions of real moral, spiritual or cosmic weight make new bubbles.
- A **serialized bubble pattern** is a collection of bubbles that share a connecting theme. Colloquially, a
  "multiverse". The Magic multiverse is one such pattern; each **plane** in the game is a bubble in it.
- **Travel is lateral, never backward.** True backward time travel always collapses. Moving between bubbles is
  lateral travel, which is what planar portals do.
- **Time runs in gravity wells**: local, regional, planetary, galactic and up. Deeper wells bend time harder.
  (Game use: delve depth.)

## The powers

- **The Firstborn**: the first and greatest beings of the Engine.
  - **The Fallen**: Firstborn who were corrupted. Canonically the *weakest* of the Firstborn, which is how they
    could be corrupted. They must act in secret, because open action draws the other Firstborn down on them.
  - **The Emperor** will readily send enough force to neutralize even a Fallen.
  - **The Firstborn leader of the Deathwatch**: arguably the most powerful souled being, counting both raw power
    and the forces at their command. Strictly apolitical, but the Fallen aren't political, so they get crushed.
  - **The Intrinsics** stay out of things unless it's a very big deal.
- **The Deathwatch**: the Engine's elite military and **cosmic immune system**. Its operatives maintain the
  Engine's health and preserve sapient species for reseeding after extinction events. A million years minimum
  training, a billion years minimum service, protected from death while serving.

## The player

- You are a **Deathwatch Initiate**. By most standards, Initiates in their million years of training already count
  as ultra-elite. Once fully **blooded and tattooed**, an Initiate is **bonded with their Watcher** and sent on
  missions; missions are the training.
- Each Initiate has their own **Watcher**, named by the player (with a default name offered, so naming is optional). Narratively the Watcher is a spy, advisor and
  scout: it scouts ahead, gathers intelligence and advises. (Game use: the guide and intel voice, scouting reports,
  hints on where to investigate.)
- A **Supervisor** oversees a set number of Initiates. Also nameable by the player, with a default name offered. In co-op, both players share one Supervisor (the host's name for the
  Supervisor is used).
- **The mission**: activity of the Nothing has been detected in the Magic multiverse. You are sent in to find its
  source. You don't know which plane it's on. You must grow strong enough, and investigate enough planes, to find
  the Fallen behind it and wreck their plans.

## The enemy: the Nothing

- **The Nothing eats the map.** Where something flourished, there is now literal nothingness: land, towns,
  resources and roads are erased. **Once it's gone, it's gone. There is no recovery.** That permanence is the true
  nastiness of the Nothing.
- Its forces are **soulless minions** of the Nothing, given power by **one of the Fallen**, who is hiding somewhere
  in this multiverse.
- **Which Fallen is unknown, by design.** The true identities of the Fallen are known only to the Primals and
  Primordials; when they fell, the minds of souled beings shifted to know them only as what they are now. They may
  have names among themselves, deliberately left unfilled, so any story can use whatever name fits. In this game
  the Fallen stays nameless (or is known only by an epithet the characters give it).
- **The Fallen's plan**: the Nothing's traditional weakness is that it moves between bubbles slowly. The Fallen
  wants to harness this multiverse's **planeswalking** to build a larger network for moving quickly between
  bubbles. Success would remove the Nothing's greatest weakness.
- **The win condition is exposure, not a duel with a god.** A Fallen is far above even a Deathwatch planeswalker.
  Your job is to make the Fallen's work too messy to hide: find the source, break their operation, and drag it
  into the open so the Fallen must withdraw or face the other Firstborn (the Emperor's forces, the Deathwatch's
  leader).

## How the story unfolds in play

1. **Arrival**: you start exploring planes, building, gathering and mastering the first Standard set. The Nothing
   is only rumor and strange signs.
2. **The Nothing arrives**: after the **first Standard set is mastered**, the Nothing starts appearing and
   competing with you for the multiverse. Fronts open; the map starts disappearing.
3. **The investigation**: the Nothing builds network nodes on planes to link them for fast transit. Each plane
   holds clues. Investigating planes, defeating the Nothing's commanders and
   closing its breaches narrows down where the source is.
4. **Exposure**: you find the Fallen's operation and make it impossible to keep secret. The Fallen's plans fail;
   the Engine's greater powers move in.
5. **After**: the Engine keeps generating new iterations. Prestige can be framed as a new mission (soul-weight
   carried forward).

## Gameplay consequences

- **Permanent erasure**: voided land never comes back. The Nothing only advances while you play, its pace is
  tunable, and closing a breach stops that breach's spread. New set planes keep arriving, so the game never runs
  out of world, but each plane you lose is lost for good.
- **The network is the target**: the Nothing's transit nodes near planar portals are the main objective on each
  plane. Left alone, linked nodes let the Nothing jump to new planes faster; destroying them yields clues and
  slows the spread.
- **The Watcher** is the player's guide and intel source (named at character creation).
- **Fortresses anchor reality**: the Nothing can only besiege a fortress, never erase it. The land around it can
  still be eaten.
